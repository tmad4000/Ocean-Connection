ABOUT THE THIRD EYE

an illuminated companion to the opening section of Zhuan Falun, Lecture Two — passage by passage: anchor line, paraphrase, and figure
How to read this. The original chapter is copyrighted and is therefore not reproduced here; it is distributed free at falundafa.org — Lecture Two (2003 translation, whose terms this companion follows). Each folio below covers one movement of the "About the Third Eye" section in order: a short anchor line quoted verbatim so you can find your place in the original, then a faithful paraphrase, a margin note, and — where a picture genuinely helps — a figure. The intended use is two windows side by side: the original text open on one side, this companion on the other. By the tradition's own account, it is the reading of the original that matters; this page is the gloss in the margin, never the scripture.
IWhy it was never explained ¶ 1 · read original
"There are a lot of other qigong masters who've talked about some things…" — opening line of the section

The section opens with a claim about perspective: the same thing looks different from different heights of cultivation, so accounts of the third eye have disagreed not because the eye differs but because the tellers stood at different levels — and none, the text says, has laid out its real structure. What follows is offered as that missing structural account.

A reading instruction disguised as a preface: expect anatomy, not poetry.
IIWhere it is ¶ 2 · read original
"The Third Eye we're talking about is actually located slightly above and between a person's eyebrows…" — on location

The seat is precise: slightly above the midpoint between the eyebrows, connected inward to the pineal gland. And the teaching immediately widens: this main eye is not alone — the body carries many eyes; every acupuncture point, it says, is an eye. The brow passage is simply the main gate.

IIIWhat ordinary eyes actually do ¶ 3–4 · read original
"This pair of eyes can fix the things in this material dimension…" — on the flesh eyes

Before explaining the inner eye, the text demotes the outer ones. The flesh eyes do not see reality; they see one dimension's surface, at one scale, and freeze it into a stable picture. They work like a camera: a lens that fixes images. The actual seeing, it insists, never happened in the eyeball at all — the image forms and is read deeper in, in the region of the pineal gland. The eyes are instruments; the seer sits behind them.

This paragraph is doing quiet philosophical work: if the eye never saw anything anyway, then "seeing without the eye" stops sounding supernatural and starts sounding like a rewiring.
IVWhat "opening" means ¶ 5–6 · read original
"When we talk about 'opening the Third Eye'…" — the definition

The definition is mechanical: to open the third eye is to open a channel so the pineal gland sees directly, bypassing the optic nerves. And the text calls modern anatomy as its witness: the front half of the pineal gland carries the complete structure of a human eye — lens-like tissue, light receptivity. Medicine calls it vestigial, an eye from an earlier age; the cultivation schools, the text says, call it an eye waiting for its channel.

VThe five visions — and where yours opens ¶ 7–9 · read original
"Buddhists talk about the five powers…" — the gradation of sight

Sight is graded into five: Flesh Vision, Celestial Vision, Wisdom Vision, Dharma Vision, Buddha Vision. The text then makes a deliberate engineering choice: practitioners' eyes are opened at Wisdom Vision. Lower — at Flesh or Celestial Vision — one would see through walls and bodies, and that traffic with this world's affairs would only disturb ordinary society and the practitioner both. Wisdom Vision sees into other dimensions instead, where nothing here can be meddled with. And crucially: those who read and cultivate in earnest receive what matters regardless of whether their eye ever shows them anything clearly.

Note the inversion of every occult sales pitch: the powers that would impress — x-ray sight, seeing through things — are precisely the ones withheld, as a mercy.
VIFirst determinant — vital essence, which is virtue ¶ 10–14 · read original
"So what determines the level of a person's Third Eye?" — the three factors begin

Three factors set the level of a person's eye, and the first is startlingly moral: the clarity of vision runs on vital essence, a refined matter derived from virtue. Children see easily because their store is intact. Adults, soaked in the age's currents — grasping, contending, deceiving — spend virtue continually, and with it the essence that would have fed the eye. The text is careful about the physics: virtue lost does not vanish, it transfers to others; and cultivation genuinely practiced replenishes what was drained. The eye's dimness, in this account, is a ledger, not a fate.

VIISecond determinant — self-opening, fright, and the True Eye ¶ 15–18 · read original
"A lot of times some people get startled when their Third Eyes…" — on first sight

The second factor concerns how the eye comes open. Some open spontaneously through their own practice — and the first experience is often fright: a sudden inward seeing, unasked-for, at night, and the startle itself slams the door again. Against this the text sets the way of a master: not merely widening the channel but forming and setting a True Eye in place for the student — with dazzling light, pressure, and brightness felt at the brow as the sign of the work in progress. Foreknowledge is the remedy the passage offers: whatever appears, remain unmoved.

The practical tone is deliberate — this is written as troubleshooting, the way a manual warns which noises during installation are normal.
VIIIThird determinant — the sub-passages ¶ 19–22 · read original
"A person sees not only through the main passageway…" — on the lesser gates

The third factor is structural: beside the main passage above the brow run sub-passageways — at other points of the face and body — and levels of cultivation open them differently. At the far end of the account stand beings of very high attainment whose whole upper face is eyes, a compound sight seeing every dimension at once. The section then closes its anatomy lesson with reassurance: the structure has now been laid out; for those whose eye stays dim, continued cultivation itself will clear it.

IXThe tunnel without end ¶ 23–25 · read original
"It's harder for a person to open his Third Eye on his own." — on the long way

For those opening unaided, the text describes the experience from inside: drilling inward along a dark passage that never seems to end — years of it, for some. The explanation given is Daoist: the body is a small universe, and the distance from brow to pineal gland, measured in the body's own inner scale, is astronomically far. The tunnel feels endless because, in the dimension being traversed, it is.

XWorlds within — the dimensional argument ¶ 26–29 · read original
"Now, we know that material things are composed…" — on scale and dimension

Not everyone meets a tunnel; some, on opening, see plains and skies — a landscape, an elsewhere. The text folds this into its picture of matter: reality is layered by particle scale, and each complete layer is a world; a qigong master's old saying about cities in a pore is retold as a statement about dimensional correspondence, not poetry. To see a layer whole is to have broken through to it. Other openings come with their own imagery — a taiji splitting open, and the like — each a doorway dressed in the school's own emblems.

XIThe seal — wanting blocks it ¶ 30 · read original
"And there are some people who really want to get their Third Eyes opened…" — the closing warning

The section ends on the teaching that governs everything before it: the desire to have the eye open is itself an attachment, and attachment gathers like a dark field before the very place that would see. The harder the wanting, the thicker the veil. The only instruction that survives this paragraph is indirect: cultivate character, read, practice — and let the eye be someone else's business.

The manuscript's last illumination is anti-illumination — the one figure in which the gold is behind the grey.
